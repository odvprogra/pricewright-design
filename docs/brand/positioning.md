# Positioning

Pricewright, its customers and its demo tenants are fictional; the positioning is written as for a
real product.

## Positioning statement

Written in Geoffrey Moore's form from _Crossing the Chasm_: a value proposition (for whom, the need,
the category, the benefit) and a differentiation (unlike the alternative, what sets it apart).

> **For** sales teams at mid-size B2B distributors **who** quote prices by hand and approve large
> discounts over chat, **Pricewright is** a quote-to-order platform **that** prices every line with
> a transparent waterfall of discounts, routes the risky ones for approval and keeps the record of
> every decision. **Unlike** spreadsheets and the quoting module bolted onto an ERP, **Pricewright**
> shows why each price is what it is, so reps quote with confidence and managers approve with the
> facts in front of them.

## Promise

Every price, explained; every decision, on the record.

## Personality

The Ledger direction ([ADR-0003](../adr/0003-the-ledger-brand-direction.md)) in three traits, each
with what it is not:

| Trait     | Is                                                    | Is not                       |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Precise   | Exact numbers, exact names, the rule that applied     | Vague, rounded, approximate  |
| Careful   | Confirms what matters, keeps history, never surprises | Slow, bureaucratic, fussy    |
| Permanent | Looks like a document you can file and audit later    | Old-fashioned, stiff, ornate |

## Name

"Wright" means a maker, as in playwright and shipwright: Pricewright is the maker of prices (D-13).
Written as one word with a capital P; never "PriceWright", "Price Wright" or "PW" in copy.
